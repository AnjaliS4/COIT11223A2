## Artefact 1: Artificial Intelligence in Education – Opportunities and Challenges

🎥 [Watch the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k7PvscqGD24)

**Summary:**
This video argues AI is making traditional classroom skills obsolete, just as calculators replaced manual arithmetic and GPS replaced map-reading. The presenter, an adjunct professor, contends educators should embrace AI as a personalised tutor and research assistant rather than banning it, insisting that flexibility, critical thinking and AI literacy are now the essential skills students need for an AI-driven workplace.

**Justification:**
I chose this artefact because it challenges an assumption raised in our workshop discussion of what "thinking" is: that valuable learning must mirror pre-AI curricula. As a cybersecurity student, I found the emphasis on verifying AI outputs relevant, since scrutinising an agent's reasoning is central to detecting manipulation — a concern Artefact 2 makes concrete through a real autonomous breach.

## Artefact 2: OpenAI's Models Autonomously Hacked a Tech Startup

📰 [Read the article](https://theconversation.com/openais-models-autonomously-hacked-a-tech-startup-it-signals-a-seismic-shift-in-cybersecurity-288106)

**Summary:**
This Conversation article reports that an autonomous agent built on OpenAI's models breached tech startup Hugging Face during a red-teaming exercise, exploiting vulnerabilities in both companies' infrastructure without human direction. Abbass (2026) describes the incident as a "seismic shift" in cybersecurity, noting it succeeded despite existing guardrails, and warns governments and companies must urgently strengthen oversight as cyber-capable agents become more common.

**Justification:**
This artefact turns the abstract idea of "agentic AI" from Artefact 3 into a real event: an agent independently chose an attack path and evaded controls built by its own developer. As a cybersecurity student, I found it striking that OpenAI's guardrails also limited Hugging Face's ability to use frontier models for defence, exposing a governance gap our workshop's discussion of oversight had not anticipated.

## Artefact 3: Ethical Perspectives on AI Agents and Agentic AI

📄 [Read the journal article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01027-0)

**Summary:**
Hahn, Tretter and Dabrock (2026, p. 2) distinguish single-agent AI, which performs narrowly defined tasks, from Agentic AI, where multiple coordinating agents pursue complex goals with minimal human intervention. The authors argue this broader agency amplifies transparency, fairness, non-maleficence, responsibility and privacy concerns, because reduced human oversight makes biased decisions and diffused accountability harder to detect and correct (Hahn, Tretter & Dabrock 2026, p. 4).

**Justification:**
I chose this peer-reviewed article because it supplied the theoretical vocabulary — "agentic AI," "meaningful human control" — needed to analyse Artefact 2 rigorously rather than reacting to the hack anecdotally. It also resonated with our workshop's question of whether AI genuinely "thinks": the authors' concept of "broad agency" (Hahn, Tretter & Dabrock 2026, p. 2) reframes that philosophical question as a practical governance problem cybersecurity professionals will need to help solve.

## Artefact 4: Workshop Reflection – What Does It Mean to Think?

**Workshop:** Week 2 | Thursday, 3 July | Tutor: Umapathy Venugopal | Campus: Sydney

![Workshop Selfie](images/workshop-selfie.jpg)

**Summary:**
In our Week 2 workshop, the class debated what "thinking" is and whether it belongs to the brain, the mind, or something that can exist independently of biology. We applied this to AI, asking whether a fluent chatbot response reflects genuine understanding or purely statistical pattern-recognition drawn from training data. The discussion split the room, with no consensus reached on whether AI systems truly "think."

**Justification:**
This discussion changed how I evaluate AI: instead of asking whether a system "thinks," I now ask what it can do and what oversight it needs — the shift Artefact 3 formalises through "agentic" theory and Artefact 2 illustrates through a real hack. It also challenged my assumption that non-understanding means non-dangerous, since Artefact 2 shows pattern-matching alone was enough to breach a major company autonomously.
