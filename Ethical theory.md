## Artefact 1: EU Data Act and EU AI Act Explained

[The EU Data Act and the Artificial Intelligence (AI) Act: new laws on data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u37w2519HK0)

### Summary of the artefact

The EU AI Act's structure is based on the risks of the AI system, and this interview explains how the regulations are applied across this spectrum from minimal risk to unacceptable risk. The penalties for non-compliance can be up to 35 million or up to 7% of worldwide turnover and the Act is extraterritorial, meaning that any non-EU business that uses AI in the EU is covered.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

This one I selected because, according to the EU announcement, new rules are in force, but this is what will actually make them happen: real tiered risk classifications and real fines. It is the “clearly named accountable authority” that Chung and Schiff (2025, p. 622) call for, actualized through the closing question regarding non-EU businesses, which indicates the sovereignty's range of effectiveness is not unlimited.

## Artefact 2: Safer and More Transparent AI — EU AI Act Transparency Rules

[Safer and more transparent AI](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/safer-and-more-transparent-ai-2026-08-02_en)

### Summary of the artefact

AI transparency rules are new in the EU, with the rules starting to be enforced on 2 August 2026 and with concrete penalties for infringements. The European Commission views the issue as a form of citizen protection from deception and manipulation in the context of the increasing integration of AI into everyday life.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

The social contract of Hobbes in actual effect: a government making strict regulations for business in the name of collective security. The EU Act provides exactly what Chung and Schiff (2025, p. 622) believe is necessary: "a legally responsible authority with clearly defined name. I wondered if this was true submission to protection or it was a form of protection, disguised as consent.

## Artefact 3: AI and the Social Contract

[AI and the Social Contract](https://doi.org/10.1609/aies.v8i1.36575)

### Summary of the artefact

Chung and Schiff (2025, p. 615) examine five social contract theories (Hobbes, Locke, Rousseau, Rawls and Nozick) and conclude that none of these theories provide an account of legitimacy or consent. They point out that Hobbes' “agreement” in the state of nature always involved fear, and not actual consent, so that it is difficult to distinguish between people reasonably consenting to be governed and being forced to submit (p. 621).

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

I selected this because it directly addresses that which our workshop left unanswered: does the state of nature really call for submission or mere excuse? One solution that Chung and Schiff (2025, p. 622) propose is to mandate that each AI system has a "legally accountable authority clearly named" which is exactly what both Artefacts 1 and 2 demonstrate in practice.

### Summary of the artefact

We have spent Week 4 in the workshop discussing the social contract theory, which Hobbes argued was better than the state of nature, and that we unwittingly agree to give control to the leader, the worker as well as the soldier. Rousseau added a qualification which Hobbes did not: legitimacy can only exist when "no one is above the rules.

### Justification on why I chose the artefact

This was a thought that stayed with me, as this is not about ancient philosophy, but this is happening right now with AI. The absence of any original contract, the same dissatisfaction that we saw with Hobbes, is what researchers found in the area of AI governance as well (Chung & Schiff 2025). Reminds me of the same excuse that history gave for submission, just with a new king.
