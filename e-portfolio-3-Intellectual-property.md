### Summary of the artefact
This video covers the four types of intellectual property protection: patents for novel inventions (life of 20 years); trademarks for brand identifiers (renewable for 10 years); for know-how (no explanation). It positions each as a distinct balancing act between rewarding a single creator and, over time, returning the creation to public. 

This relates directly to the first slide of our workshop, which discussed how granting time-limited rights to creators encourages creativity, yet it is the public domain that brings the greatest benefit to society once innovations enter it. Prior to the workshop, I believed IP protection existed only to reward creators. That’s changes when we discussed the compromise in slide3: protection is not a reward but a compromise between individual incentive and public benefit. 

### Justification on why I choose the artefact 
I selected this video because, as an ICT professional, I will have to decide what type of protection to use for a product: either public, time-limited protection, or a trade secret such as the Coca-Cola formula. In practice, if I a developing software in my job, deciding whether to make an algorithm open source or treat it as confidential IP is not only a technical and legal choice but also an ethical one: whether it should benefit the public or be more profitable for the company.
